## 0. THE CHARTER — unchanged

The charter of this track is at the head of `content/_ledger-aeneis.md` §0 and
at the head of `content/aeneis-al1.js`, and Librī IX–XII obey it as written:

* **§0.1** the whole S1–S12 ladder is open;
* **§0.2** what is graded is the VOCABULARY — ≤10 new content cards per
  capitulum, each pictured and recycled ≥3× inside its own capitulum;
* **§0.3** *cōpia commūnis* (Ørberg *Familia Rōmāna* level) is assumed unglossed;
* **§0.4** poetic diction is PRE-TAUGHT before any authentic line uses it;
* **§0.5** each liber's LAST capitulum closes with *Verba Vergiliī ipsīus*;
* **§0.6** categories: CARD · TAUGHT · CONTEXT · NŌMEN PROPRIUM · FORMULA ·
  VERBA VERGILIĪ.

### 0.7 What Librī IX–XII assume from Librī I–V, and what they do NOT

These four librī were authored **in parallel with Librī VI–VIII** and therefore
treat **only a1–a20 as free**. Nothing from a21–a32 is assumed anywhere: every
word Librī IX–XII need that Librī VI–VIII might also have taught (`bellum` is
a5's, `hasta` is a5's, `castra` is a5's, `hostis` is a7's, `clāmor` is a7's,
`fugit` is a7's, `pietās` is a8's) is taken from Librī I–V or carded afresh.
Proper names are the one place the two waves are certain to overlap — **Turnus,
Latīnus, Lāvīnia, Pallās, Ēvander, Camilla, Mezentius** — and by charter §0.6 a
name is free against the cap and glossed at first use, so both waves may gloss
the same name without either being wrong. The integrator need do nothing.

### 0.8 THE BALTEUS — one visual variable across three librī

The four librī of this part share ONE running picture, and it is the object
Vergil hangs the end of his poem on: the sword-belt Turnus strips from Pallās
at 10,495–500 and is still wearing at 12,941. The art library has no belt prop,
so the belt is carried as **a single colour variable on one figure**:

| liber | Turnus is drawn | why |
|---|---|---|
| IX (a33–a36) | `robeColor: RUTULUS, mantleColor: CANUS` — blue tunic, **white** cloak, no gold anywhere | he does not have the belt yet |
| X a37–a38 | the same white | still not his |
| X a39 onward | `mantleColor: GOLD` — one variable, changed once | he takes it on that page |
| XI (a41–a44) | gold | he keeps it |
| XII a47 | gold, and **Aenēās sees it** | the poem's last turn |

Aenēās' own mantle has been GOLD since a1 and never changes; what changes is
that the RUTULIAN acquires gold he did not have. Pallās wears the same gold in
a38, alone in the frame, as the card `v_balteus`. By the time a47 says *Aenēās
balteum vidit*, the learner has been looking at that gold band for two librī.

**LEDGER CORRECTION, applied.** `content/aeneis-al9.js` originally drew Turnus
with `mantleColor: GOLD` from a33 on, which would have spent the gold before
the belt existed and left a47 with nothing to point at. The file was corrected

## a34 · Nīsus et Euryalus · fons Aen. 9,168–449

**CARDS (4):** amīcitia · cōnsilium · silentium · lūna
(`v_luna` is the `nightSky` background **and nothing else** — bgNightSky draws
the moon itself at 318,58, so the card is the background; `v_silentium` is the
same sky with tents and one figure in `pose: 'sleep'`, which is what tells the
two apart).

**THE OMISSION, DECLARED.** Aen. 9,314–366 — fifty lines of throat-cutting
through a sleeping camp — is reduced to ONE SENTENCE, and the reduction is
announced in Latin on the page:

> *Per castra hostium tacitē iērunt. Quid ibi fēcerint, Vergilius nārrat: nōs
> autem hīc tacēmus.*

That is the al3/al4 EDITORIAL-VOICE DEVICE (a12's *Plūra Achaemenidēs nōn
dīcit*, a16's *Vergilius plūra dīcit, nōs autem hīc tacēmus*) used where it is
licensed: exactly where DESIGN §8 forbids showing. The learner is not deceived —
he is told that something is in the poem and is not on this page.
**THE SPOILS GO WITH THE SLAUGHTER.** Vergil's Euryalus is betrayed by
Messāpus' captured helmet (9,365–366 · 9,373–374). A helmet taken as loot needs
the loot, so this file gives the helmet **no provenance at all**: *Euryalus
galeam gerēbat, quae sub lūnā lūcem reddidit.* Omission, never alteration —
nothing false is asserted, the fact that betrays him is Vergil's own, and where
the helmet came from is simply not said. `galea` is TAUGHT and never carded
(**MISSING ART**: no helmet prop, as al5 already reported).

**THE TWO DEATHS — the a16 REFUSAL DEVICE, in full.** Aen. 9,431–445 is the
most beautiful violent passage in Latin and none of it is on these pages. What
is on them is this, and nothing else:

> *Ambō cecidērunt. Quō modō cecidērunt, hīc nōn nārrātur: Vergilius plūra
> dīcit, nōs autem hīc tacēmus.*
> *Sed audī: Vergilius eōs semper vīvere dīcit. Nam poēta, cum dē amīcīs canit,
> eōs ē morte in versūs suōs tollit.*

The death is ACKNOWLEDGED (DESIGN §8: "acknowledged, dignified"), the mechanism
is REFUSED OUT LOUD, and the refusal is itself in Latin so the learner can see
that something was left out on purpose. **There is no wound, no weapon, no
body and no blood anywhere in a34.** The scene under the refusal is the a16
picture language with the pyre taken out: night, an empty wood, and a `columna`
for the poet's voice.
What replaces the deaths is what Vergil replaces them with two lines later —
and a34's LAST PAGE POINTS FORWARD TO IT: *Quid dē eīs dīxerit, in fīne huius
librī legēs.* a36 keeps the promise.

**THE CRY — Aen. 9,427, first half, as a FORMULA.** The line is *"mē, mē, adsum
quī fēcī, in mē convertite ferrum"*. This file quotes:

> *"Mē, mē! Adsum quī fēcī!"*

and the page after it says, in the liber's own device, *Plūra Nīsus dīcit, et
Vergilius plūra scrībit; nōs autem hīc tacēmus.* Precedent: a1's *"Quōs ego—"*,
which this ledger already registers as a FORMULA and not as a VERBA VERGILIĪ
block — a partial line, given as what it is, with the break marked. Two rules
meet here and both are kept: the track's "no fragment that cannot be parsed"
(the quoted words parse — an accusative of exclamation, then a complete clause
*adsum quī fēcī*) and DESIGN §8 (the half of the line that names the sword and
asks for it is not put in a child's mouth). It is therefore **not counted** as
one of the liber's authentic hexameters.

**TAUGHT:** glōria · tacitē · galea · eques (re-carded in a43).
**NŌMINA PROPRIA:** — (Nīsus, Euryalus and Ascanius all met in a19/a3).
**FORMULA:** `Mē, mē! Adsum quī fēcī!` (Aen. 9,427a).
**CONTEXT:** onus (a20's) · impedītus (a19's) · respiciēns (a16's `respicit`) ·
prōcurrit · pāret.
**OMITTED ENTIRELY:** 9,314–366 · 9,359–366 (the spoils) · 9,431–445 (the two
deaths as Vergil paints them) · **9,473–502, the mother's lament**, because it
is built on her son's head carried on a spear (9,465–472) — horror imagery,
DESIGN §8. She appears only where Vergil first puts her: alive, asleep, and the
reason Nīsus tells his friend to stay behind.

## a39 · Turnus et Pallās · fons Aen. 10,439–509

**CARDS (3):** Turnus (re-carded from a33, same picture) · victor · lūget.

**PALLĀS' DEATH — the a16 REFUSAL DEVICE, first of this liber's two uses.**
Aen. 10,474–489 is one spear through a shield and a body. On the page there is
this and nothing else:

> *Duo iuvenēs concurrunt. Quō modō pugnāverint, hīc nōn nārrātur: Vergilius

> *Pallās cecidit.*

No wound, no weapon, no body, no blood. `Pallās cecidit.` stands **alone on its

**AND THE POET'S OWN VERDICT** (10,501–505) is kept, in graded prose and NOT as
a quotation, because a sentence about a man buying a day back is worth more to a
child in words he can read: *Nescit mēns hominum quid futūrum sit. Victor hodiē
gaudet; sed veniet diēs quō hunc balteum magnō pretiō ēmptum esse dīcet.*

iuvenis sustulit et umerō suō imposuit.* From this page on Turnus is drawn

balteum … in librō duodecimō iterum eum vidēbis.*

## a40 · Īra Aenēae temperāta · fons Aen. 10,633–908 (+ 10,467–469a)

**CARDS (4):** Mezentius · Lausus · parcit · reddit.

**ĪRA AENĒAE IS OMITTED ENTIRE, AND NOT REFUSED.** Aen. 10,510–605 — the killing
spree that follows the news of Pallās, including the four young men taken alive
for a human sacrifice — is the darkest thing the hero of this poem does, and no
part of the liber's arc needs it. It is therefore simply **NOT ON THE PAGE**: no
refusal sentence, no *nōs hīc tacēmus*, nothing. The device is expensive and
this liber spends it exactly twice.
The omission is not a hole, **because Vergil himself supplies the bridge**: at
10,633–688 Iūnō draws Turnus off the field with a phantom, so the man Aenēās is
hunting is not there to be found. What the capitulum shows instead is what the
brief asks for — his grief (*tacuit — deinde lacrimāvit*) and his resolve.

**LAUSUS' DEATH — the a16 REFUSAL DEVICE, second and last use:**

> *Lausus tamen nōn cessit. Quō modō ceciderit, hīc nōn nārrātur: Vergilius
> plūra dīcit, nōs autem hīc tacēmus.*

**Everything after it is Aen. 10,821–832 almost entire**, because it is the best
thing in the liber and completely safe: Aenēās sees the boy's face, groans,
remembers his own father, takes NOTHING (*Arma tua tibi manent: nihil auferō*),
lifts him himself, and gives him back to his people. Father and son wear the
SAME IRON, which is the capitulum's point — and a SONUS hazard at tile size, so
`Mezentius` and `Lausus` are never offered together.
**MEZENTIUS' OWN END (10,833–908) IS OMITTED ENTIRELY**: he goes back to look
for his son and the liber closes. Vergil's last hundred lines are named as
existing and not retold — *Plūra Vergilius nārrat; hīc autem liber decimus fīnem
habet.*
**MŌRĀLE:** *etiam hostis patrem amāvit — et hostis alter id intellēxit. Quī
victō parcit et corpus reddit, is vērē victor est.*
**TAUGHT:** claudicat · ingemit.
