### 0.7 What Librī IX–XII assume from Librī I–V, and what they do NOT

These four librī were authored **in parallel with Librī VI–VIII** and therefore
treat **only a1–a20 as free**. Nothing from a21–a32 is assumed anywhere: every
word Librī IX–XII need that Librī VI–VIII might also have taught (`bellum` is
a5's, `hasta` is a5's, `castra` is a5's, `hostis` is a7's, `clāmor` is a7's,
`fugit` is a7's, `pietās` is a8's) is taken from Librī I–V or carded afresh.
Proper names are the one place the two waves are certain to overlap — **Turnus,
Latīnus, Lāvīnia, Pallās, Ēvander, Camilla, Mezentius** — and by charter §0.6 a
name is free against the cap and glossed at first use, so both waves may gloss
the same name without either being wrong. The integrator need do nothing.

## a33 · Castra oppugnāta · fons Aen. 9,1–167

**CARDS (6)**

| lexeme | how |
|---|---|
| Turnus | card `v_turnus` — a Rutulian in **steel blue** with a **gold mantle**, the colour pair that marks him out for four librī. NŌMEN PROPRIUM, free against the cap, carded because the trial needs his picture |
| vāllum | card `v_vallum` — the `cityWall` actor alone, nobody on it and nothing beside it |
| custōs | card `v_custos` — the same wall AT NIGHT with one man awake in front of it. The night is what tells this card from `vāllum` at tile size |
| nympha | card `v_nympha` — **MISSING ART, composed**: no nymph actor exists, so the ships-turned-goddesses of 9,117–122 are what the text calls them, *deae maris fōrmā puellae* — `person` role 'woman', veil off, sea-green, standing LOW IN THE WATER (y 226) so the `sea` background's waterline cuts them at the waist. No tail, no fish, nothing invented |
| oppugnat | card `v_oppugnat` (two Rutulī pointing at a wall they are not on) |
| dēfendit | card `v_defendit` (two Trōiānī behind their shields and NO wall — a wall would make this a second `vāllum`) |

**TAUGHT (glossed, no card):** abest (`ab-est: nōn adest; ↔ adest`) · agmen ·
incendit · officium · mīrus · aufert · pīnus.
**NŌMINA PROPRIA:** Īris · Rutulī · Māter deōrum · Iovem (the oblique stem of
a5's Iuppiter, glossed as such).
**CONTEXT:** puppis (a12's) · fūnis (a16's) · mergitur (a1's) · natat · restat ·
tot … quot.

**B RATING.** Nobody is hurt anywhere in this capitulum, and the assault is
DESIGN §8's own recipe: *hastae in scūta cadunt*, a closed gate, a wall that
holds. The ships are not destroyed but CHANGED — the gentlest miracle in the
poem — and the text says so in the purpose clause itself: *nōn ut pereant, sed
ut in nymphās mūtentur.* Turnus' misreading of the omen (9,126–158) is kept
compressed and NOT commented on: he is wrong, Vergil lets him be wrong out
loud, and so does this file.
**OMITTED:** 9,14–24 · 9,176 ff. (held for a34) · the catalogue of 9,32–46.

## a34 · Nīsus et Euryalus · fons Aen. 9,168–449

**CARDS (4):** amīcitia · cōnsilium · silentium · lūna
(`v_luna` is the `nightSky` background **and nothing else** — bgNightSky draws
the moon itself at 318,58, so the card is the background; `v_silentium` is the
same sky with tents and one figure in `pose: 'sleep'`, which is what tells the
two apart).

**THE OMISSION, DECLARED.** Aen. 9,314–366 — fifty lines of throat-cutting
through a sleeping camp — is reduced to ONE SENTENCE, and the reduction is
announced in Latin on the page:

> *Per castra hostium tacitē iērunt. Quid ibi fēcerint, Vergilius nārrat: nōs
> autem hīc tacēmus.*

That is the al3/al4 EDITORIAL-VOICE DEVICE (a12's *Plūra Achaemenidēs nōn
dīcit*, a16's *Vergilius plūra dīcit, nōs autem hīc tacēmus*) used where it is
licensed: exactly where DESIGN §8 forbids showing. The learner is not deceived —
he is told that something is in the poem and is not on this page.
**THE SPOILS GO WITH THE SLAUGHTER.** Vergil's Euryalus is betrayed by
Messāpus' captured helmet (9,365–366 · 9,373–374). A helmet taken as loot needs
the loot, so this file gives the helmet **no provenance at all**: *Euryalus
galeam gerēbat, quae sub lūnā lūcem reddidit.* Omission, never alteration —
nothing false is asserted, the fact that betrays him is Vergil's own, and where
the helmet came from is simply not said. `galea` is TAUGHT and never carded
(**MISSING ART**: no helmet prop, as al5 already reported).

**THE TWO DEATHS — the a16 REFUSAL DEVICE, in full.** Aen. 9,431–445 is the
most beautiful violent passage in Latin and none of it is on these pages. What
is on them is this, and nothing else:

> *Ambō cecidērunt. Quō modō cecidērunt, hīc nōn nārrātur: Vergilius plūra
> dīcit, nōs autem hīc tacēmus.*
> *Sed audī: Vergilius eōs semper vīvere dīcit. Nam poēta, cum dē amīcīs canit,
> eōs ē morte in versūs suōs tollit.*

The death is ACKNOWLEDGED (DESIGN §8: "acknowledged, dignified"), the mechanism
is REFUSED OUT LOUD, and the refusal is itself in Latin so the learner can see
that something was left out on purpose. **There is no wound, no weapon, no
body and no blood anywhere in a34.** The scene under the refusal is the a16
picture language with the pyre taken out: night, an empty wood, and a `columna`
for the poet's voice.
What replaces the deaths is what Vergil replaces them with two lines later —
and a34's LAST PAGE POINTS FORWARD TO IT: *Quid dē eīs dīxerit, in fīne huius
librī legēs.* a36 keeps the promise.

**THE CRY — Aen. 9,427, first half, as a FORMULA.** The line is *"mē, mē, adsum
quī fēcī, in mē convertite ferrum"*. This file quotes:

> *"Mē, mē! Adsum quī fēcī!"*

and the page after it says, in the liber's own device, *Plūra Nīsus dīcit, et
Vergilius plūra scrībit; nōs autem hīc tacēmus.* Precedent: a1's *"Quōs ego—"*,
which this ledger already registers as a FORMULA and not as a VERBA VERGILIĪ
block — a partial line, given as what it is, with the break marked. Two rules
meet here and both are kept: the track's "no fragment that cannot be parsed"
(the quoted words parse — an accusative of exclamation, then a complete clause
*adsum quī fēcī*) and DESIGN §8 (the half of the line that names the sword and
asks for it is not put in a child's mouth). It is therefore **not counted** as
one of the liber's authentic hexameters.

**TAUGHT:** glōria · tacitē · galea · eques (re-carded in a43).
**NŌMINA PROPRIA:** — (Nīsus, Euryalus and Ascanius all met in a19/a3).
**FORMULA:** `Mē, mē! Adsum quī fēcī!` (Aen. 9,427a).
**CONTEXT:** onus (a20's) · impedītus (a19's) · respiciēns (a16's `respicit`) ·
prōcurrit · pāret.
**OMITTED ENTIRELY:** 9,314–366 · 9,359–366 (the spoils) · 9,431–445 (the two
deaths as Vergil paints them) · **9,473–502, the mother's lament**, because it
is built on her son's head carried on a spear (9,465–472) — horror imagery,
DESIGN §8. She appears only where Vergil first puts her: alive, asleep, and the
reason Nīsus tells his friend to stay behind.

## a35 · Turnus in castrīs · fons Aen. 9,503–777

**CARDS (3):** frāter (two of the same figure, same size, side by side — the
sameness IS the word) · virtūs (one man, a whole crowd, a city behind them;
never offered in SONUS against a33's `Turnus`, which is one man on an empty
plain) · cēdit (three shields facing right, three men walking left).

**PROWESS AS DESIGN §8 PRESCRIBES IT.** The greatest fighter in the poem is
shown by a WALL OF SHIELDS GIVING GROUND, and the word for it is `cēdit`:
*scūta ante sē tenent et pedem referunt, sed terga nōn vertunt.* Nobody in the
capitulum is struck.
**KEPT, and it is the seed of Liber XII:** Aen. 9,756–761, Vergil's own verdict
— had Turnus thought to open the gate to his own men, that day would have ended
the war. The capitulum's mōrāle is built on it: *ūna virtūs Turnī magna est;
ūnum cōnsilium eī dēest.*

**TAUGHT:** fragor · audet · exercitus.
**NŌMEN PROPRIUM:** Mnēstheus.
**MISSING ART, reported:** no battering ram, no ladder, no siege tower. The
assault of 9,503–529 is composed from `cityWall`, `fire`, `turris` (the
library's own tower prop) and men with shields.
**OMITTED (file header ruling 4):** the men on the falling tower (9,530–545 —
the tower falls as a THING and who was on it is not said) · **the deaths of
Pandarus and Bitias (9,672–755)**: the brothers open their gate, Turnus gets
inside, the gate shuts behind him, and the narrative follows Turnus. Their fate
is simply not on the page — omission, not refusal, because the refusal device
is expensive and is spent only on the two deaths the liber is ABOUT ·
9,569–671 (Numānus, Helenor, Lycus, the whole aristeia).

## a36 · Turnus effugit · fons Aen. 9,778–818 (+ 9,446–447)

**CARDS (3):** carmen (a `columna` and a speaking figure with 📜 — the visual
grammar a4 gives to "verba Vergiliī") · rīpa (**MISSING ART, composed**: the
bank is `harundo` and a `truncus` on the near side of the `river` background,
with no people) · salit (`pose: 'arms-up'` at the water's edge, the only leap
the library can draw).

**THE QUOTATION — Aen. 9,446–447, TWO whole hexameters:**

```
Fortūnātī ambō! sī quid mea carmina possunt,
nūlla diēs umquam memorī vōs eximet aevō,
```

They belong to the middle of a34 and are read at the END of the liber, exactly
as a4 quotes the proem, a12 the departure and a16 the queen's foreknowledge:
**the closing quotation must land on the LIBER's picture, and this liber's
picture is neither the rampart nor the river but the two friends.** a34's last
page promises them out loud and a36 keeps the promise; the frame page says so
in Latin (*Hīc enim Vergilius nārrāre dēsinit et ipse loquitur: poēta ad duōs
amīcōs*).
**Line 448 is NOT quoted** (*dum domus Aenēae Capitōlī immōbile saxum*): it
needs the Capitol, which this track has not built.
Everything in 446–447 is in hand at the point of quotation — `possunt` is a20's
own gloss (from 5,231 *possunt, quia posse videntur*, and the gloss says so out
loud); `diēs`, `nūllus` and `mea` are cōpia commūnis; `carmen` is this
capitulum's own card, taught on the page before; `fortūnātus`, `ambō`, `memor`,
`aevum` and `eximet` are glossed on the quotation pages themselves.
OCT/Mynors text with quantity marks added and NOT ONE LETTER CHANGED.

**VERBA VERGILIĪ (Aen. 9,446–447):** fortūnātus · ambō · sī quid · carmina ·
possunt · nūlla diēs · umquam · memor · aevum · eximet.
**TAUGHT:** sūdor.
**NŌMEN PROPRIUM:** Tiberis.
**B RATING.** Aen. 9,815–818 is kept whole **except for *ablūta caede*** — the
Tiber washing the slaughter off him. What is left is Vergil's own picture and
nothing invented: the yellow river takes him, lifts him on gentle waves, and
sends him back to his friends glad.

**PROBĀTIŌ b_al9 — Amīcitia:** TWO `sententia` phases and nothing else — no
sorting, no crossing, no chase. Six hand-authored items each (CASTRA from
a33/a35, AMĪCITIA from a34/a36), hp 5+5 over 55+55 s, the al4 tuning, because
al4 is the track's other liber built on a death and the two should feel alike.
Four ablātīvī absolūtī, two purpose clauses, a relative clause and an indirect
question across the twelve items; every gap is a picturable content lexeme in
the nominative. The last item of the liber is *Rogās quid amīcōs servāverit?
____ Vergiliī eōs servāvit* — answer `carmen`, under the scene of the quotation.
