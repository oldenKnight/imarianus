## Regiō X · PORTUS (S10) — `content/fabulae-r10.js`

Stage ceiling: everything S1–S9 **plus the passive voice and the deponents
`loquitur` / `sequitur`**. Still forbidden and avoided throughout: the
subjunctive (S11), accusative + īnfīnītīvus, ablative absolute and the
gerundive (S12).

Five rulings taken in this region, all recorded because Regiōnēs XI–XII
stand on them:

* **THE PASSIVE IS TAUGHT ON ONE KNOWN VERB AT A TIME, WITH THE ACTIVE
  BESIDE IT IN THE SAME FRAME** — the device Regiō VI used for the
  imperfect (`iam habitat; ōlim habitābat`), now `aquila videt → vulpēs ab
  aquilā vidētur`. **No new verb is ever introduced in the passive.**
  The one apparent exception, `frangit`, arrives ACTIVE first, in the
  fox's own question (`“Cūr amīcitiam frangis?”`), one page before the
  narrator says `amīcitia frangitur`.
* **THE AGENT (`ā` / `ab` + ablative) IS PART OF THE LESSON.** Regiō IV
  shipped `ab` as "away from"; the agent sense is new, so it carries its own
  `nova` (`quis videt? aquila: “ab aquilā”`) and is then used eleven times,
  always with an animal the picture shows.
* **THE NARRATIVE TENSE STAYS THE PRESENT.** The perfect is legal from S7,
  but the whole track has told its fables in the narrative present since
  Regiō I and the genre wants it. The perfect therefore appears exactly
  ONCE, as the perfect PASSIVE the fable's own logic asks for — f29
  `columba ā formīcā servāta est` — glossed against the present passive
  three lines above it.
* **THE DEPONENTS ARE RECEPTIVE ONLY** and are glossed as synonyms of verbs
  the learner has (`dīcit = loquitur`). Neither is ever set against a real
  passive: a learner who reads `loquitur` AS a passive still reads the
  sentence correctly, which is why CURRICULUM names these two.
* **B-RATING (DESIGN §8), two softenings, both flagged for line-audit:**
  f28's eagle does not feed the cub to her chicks and the ember does not
  roast them — the fire only THREATENS the nest and the cub goes home;
  f30's ass is not beaten, he is found out, and everybody (including the
  ass) laughs.

**MISSING ART, reported and not worked around (third time — Regiōnēs IV and
V filed the same report about `via`):** the region is called PORTUS and the
word `portus` is taught NOWHERE in it. A `ship` prop and a `sea` background
exist, but none of the three fables CURRICULUM §1 assigns this region goes
near the water, and reaching a harbour would mean either a scene no page
needs or a rewritten fable. The map header carries the name; the content
does not.
