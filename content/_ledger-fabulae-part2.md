## Regiō X · PORTUS (S10) — `content/fabulae-r10.js`

Stage ceiling: everything S1–S9 **plus the passive voice and the deponents
`loquitur` / `sequitur`**. Still forbidden and avoided throughout: the
subjunctive (S11), accusative + īnfīnītīvus, ablative absolute and the
gerundive (S12).

Five rulings taken in this region, all recorded because Regiōnēs XI–XII
stand on them:

* **THE PASSIVE IS TAUGHT ON ONE KNOWN VERB AT A TIME, WITH THE ACTIVE
  BESIDE IT IN THE SAME FRAME** — the device Regiō VI used for the
  imperfect (`iam habitat; ōlim habitābat`), now `aquila videt → vulpēs ab
  aquilā vidētur`. **No new verb is ever introduced in the passive.**
  The one apparent exception, `frangit`, arrives ACTIVE first, in the
  fox's own question (`“Cūr amīcitiam frangis?”`), one page before the
  narrator says `amīcitia frangitur`.
* **THE AGENT (`ā` / `ab` + ablative) IS PART OF THE LESSON.** Regiō IV
  shipped `ab` as "away from"; the agent sense is new, so it carries its own
  `nova` (`quis videt? aquila: “ab aquilā”`) and is then used eleven times,
  always with an animal the picture shows.
* **THE NARRATIVE TENSE STAYS THE PRESENT.** The perfect is legal from S7,
  but the whole track has told its fables in the narrative present since
  Regiō I and the genre wants it. The perfect therefore appears exactly
  ONCE, as the perfect PASSIVE the fable's own logic asks for — f29
  `columba ā formīcā servāta est` — glossed against the present passive
  three lines above it.
* **THE DEPONENTS ARE RECEPTIVE ONLY** and are glossed as synonyms of verbs
  the learner has (`dīcit = loquitur`). Neither is ever set against a real
  passive: a learner who reads `loquitur` AS a passive still reads the
  sentence correctly, which is why CURRICULUM names these two.
* **B-RATING (DESIGN §8), two softenings, both flagged for line-audit:**
  f28's eagle does not feed the cub to her chicks and the ember does not
  roast them — the fire only THREATENS the nest and the cub goes home;
  f30's ass is not beaten, he is found out, and everybody (including the
  ass) laughs.

**MISSING ART, reported and not worked around (third time — Regiōnēs IV and
V filed the same report about `via`):** the region is called PORTUS and the
word `portus` is taught NOWHERE in it. A `ship` prop and a `sea` background
exist, but none of the three fables CURRICULUM §1 assigns this region goes
near the water, and reaching a harbour would mean either a scene no page
needs or a rewritten fable. The map header carries the name; the content
does not.

## Regiō XI · TEMPLUM (S11) — `content/fabulae-r11.js`

Stage ceiling: everything S1–S10 **plus the SUBJUNCTIVE** — purpose with
`ut`/`nē`, result with `tam … ut`, cum-clauses, and the indirect question.
Still forbidden and avoided throughout: accusative + īnfīnītīvus, the
ablative absolute and the gerundive. All three are Regiō XII's, and this
region leaves them alone deliberately so that the finale has something of
its own.

Six rulings, all of which Regiō XII then stands on:

* **THE SUBJUNCTIVE IS TAUGHT ON ONE KNOWN VERB AT A TIME, WITH THE
  INDICATIVE BESIDE IT IN THE GLOSS** — the device Regiō VI used for the
  imperfect and Regiō X for the passive, now `grūs venit → lupus clāmat ut
  grūs veniat`. **No new verb is ever introduced in the subjunctive.** The
  one new verb the region's grammar needs, `extrahit`, arrives in a purpose
  clause ONLY after the indicative has stood on the page beside it.
* **`ut` AND `nē` ARE GLOSSED AS A CONTRAST, IN ONE FRAME**: introduced two
  pages apart on the SAME verb and the same picture (`ut grūs veniat` ·
  `nē grūs timeat`), so the learner meets the pair and not two unrelated
  words.
* **SEQUENCE OF TENSES IS OBEYED AND NEVER FUDGED** (LATIN-STYLE §4 names
  it). The narrative tense of this track is the PRESENT, so every purpose,
  result and indirect question here takes the PRESENT subjunctive. The
  region contains exactly ONE historical cum-clause and it sits in a
  page that is past on both sides — `Ōlim, cum senex in silvā ambulāret,
  onus magnum portābat` — an imperfect main verb with an imperfect
  subjunctive under it. Nowhere is a present main verb given a past
  subjunctive or the reverse.
* **THE PASSIVE SUBJUNCTIVE IS USED TWICE, AND ONLY IN f32** (`nē
  capiantur`, `nē videantur`). It is not a new construction — it is Regiō
  X's voice inside Regiō XI's mood — and it is glossed off the passive the
  learner already reads (`capitur → nē capiantur`). It is also what keeps
  the fable inside the B-rating.
* **THE BONE IS NOT IN f31, ON PURPOSE** (departure from the fable as
  commonly told). Aesop's wolf has a BONE in his throat; the Latin is
  `os, ossis`, a homograph of `ōs, ōris` — the MOUTH, which Regiō III
  taught and which the page needs three times over (`caput in ōs lupī`,
  `ex ōre lupī`). Ørberg would not put `os in ōre` in front of a learner,
  and no bone exists in the art library to picture one with. What sticks
  is `cibus`, free since Regiō IV, pictured, and the exact thing the crane
  then pulls out. The punchline survives word for word: `satis est quod
  caput ex ōre lupī salvum extrāxistī`.
* **B-RATING (DESIGN §8), three decisions, ALL FLAGGED FOR LINE-AUDIT:**
  f31's wolf never threatens to bite the crane's head off (the classical
  wolf boasts that he COULD have) — he simply refuses to pay and observes
  that the head came out safe, which is the same moral without the menace;
  f32's stork CHASES and NOTHING IS EATEN ON-PAGE, the frogs dive, the
  stork walks the bank, and the fable ends on what the frogs have lost;
  f33's Mors is A PERSON AND A CALM ONE — see the note below, which is the
  most-flagged decision in this region.

**TEMPLUM IS TAUGHT.** Regiōnēs IV, V and X each had to report that their
own name could not be pictured (`via`, `portus`). This one can: f32's frogs
take their demand to a TEMPLE, with the `āra` Regiō X built standing in
front of it, and `templum` is a vocabulary card.

### MORS — the whole handling, recorded (DESIGN §8, and the line-audit's business)

`Mors` is drawn with the SAME `person` actor as everybody else in this
track, at the same scale, on the same ground line. **No scythe, no
skeleton, no hood, no shadow, no dark background.** She is a veiled woman
in a dark blue robe:

    { role: 'woman', robeColor: '#4d6c8a', mantleColor: '#3d5670',
      veil: true, hair: 'long', hairColor: '#3a2417', beard: 'none' }

defined ONCE as a module-level constant and applied through a `mors()`
helper, so that every frame she stands in is demonstrably the same figure —
a reader must never wonder whether this is the same person. `#4d6c8a` is
the indigo `actors-person.js` reserves for veils and royal trim; the mantle
is one step darker. Nothing else in the track wears it.

What she DOES on the page is the other half of it: she walks up, she
stands, she asks one question (`Mors rogat cūr senex clāmet`), she lifts
the bundle, and she leaves. She never touches the old man. The fable's own
joke carries the beat — the man calls her and then wants his firewood
lifted — and the mōrāle states it without a shadow on it: `senex Mortem
vocat, sed Mortem vidēre nōn cupit`. **She is HELD OUT OF SONUS** (she is a
person and so is `senex`; a learner who hears one and taps the other has
heard correctly) and **out of the boss quiz** (a picture test, and hers is
a person), but she is recycled in AENIGMATA, in both written sets, and in
the boss's clāmor, where the SENTENCE decides.

### A note on the FREE cards, and the ≥3 rule

Eight of this region's twenty-four vocab cards carry words the learner
already has (`lupus cibus silva arbor mōns rīvus timet rāna āra onus
fessus`), and a self-check that counts occurrences will report most of them
as appearing fewer than three times in their own capitulum. **That is
correct and intended.** LATIN-STYLE §2 binds the ≥3 rule to NEW lexemes;
these are revision cards, kept for board composition — SONUS needs
same-class distractors that are not pictures of one another, and the
caterva phase draws its tiles from the region's cards. Regiō X set the
precedent explicitly (`arbor` — "FREE (f1). Card kept: the nest is in it").
Every NEW lexeme in the three tables above clears ≥3 in its own capitulum.

Two of them are worth the line-audit's eye all the same: **f31 carries
`arbor` and `mōns` cards for words its story never uses at all** (they are
there so that the `mōns`/`rīvus` place-board has two pictures that are not
each other). If the audit would rather have no card than a card for an
absent word, drop both from f31's `vocab` and rebuild the fourth SONUS item
around `grūs`/`lupus`/`cibus`.

### THE STAGING OF THE CRANE AT THE WOLF'S MOUTH (measured, and a defect caught)

The first draft of f31 put the two animals side by side at roughly equal
scale. Rendered at the real 440 px story width this was WRONG: the crane
actor is drawn a third taller than the wolf, so its beak sailed clear over
the wolf's skull while its BODY covered his muzzle — the frame read "a wolf
about to bite a bird's flank", which is the one thing the B-rating forbids
and the exact opposite of the fable. The numbers that fix it come from the
art itself:

* the `wolf` 'angry' pose is the ONE pose in this set with an open mouth
  (`open = pose === 'angry' || pose === 'leap'`); its head group sits at
  actor-local (34, −50) and the gape is drawn from head-local x 8→26,
  y −2→12, so the open jaw centres on actor-local (+50, −44) — mirrored to
  (−50, −44) when flipped. **44 units above the ground line.**
* the crane's head sits at actor-local (18, −76) and its long beak runs out
  to head-local (hr·2.6, +3) with hr 7, so the beak TIP is at actor-local
  (+40.4, −74). **74 units above the ground line.**

`74·sGrūs = 44·sLupus`, i.e. **sGrūs ≈ 0.6·sLupus**, is therefore the only
way these two heads can meet on this ground line. Equal scales cannot draw
this picture. The region takes the CLOSE-UP rather than a shrunken bird:
the wolf comes forward to s 1.7 (hindquarters off the right edge, which is
what a close-up is) and the crane keeps a natural s 1.01 — then
74·1.01 ≈ 44·1.7, and `craneX + 40.4·1.01 = 296 − 50·1.7` puts the tip in
the middle of the open jaw. **The wolf then holds still** — same x, same
scale, same pose across `f31_aperit → f31_caput → f31_extrahit` — and only
the crane moves (132 apart · 176 in · 146 backing out with the dish).
Verified by rendering at 440 px, 96 px and 60 px, not by arithmetic.

## Regiō XII · FORUM (S12) — `content/fabulae-r12.js` — **THE FINALE**

Stage ceiling: everything S1–S11 **plus the three constructions this
region owns** — the ACCUSATIVE + ĪNFĪNĪTĪVUS (indirect statement), the
ABLATIVE ABSOLUTE, and the GERUNDIVE, the last deliberately LIGHT (three
uses in the whole region). Nothing is withheld any more: this is the top
of the ladder and the region spends the whole of it.

Seven rulings:

* **THE THREE NEW CONSTRUCTIONS ARRIVE ONE AT A TIME, ON KNOWN VERBS,
  WITH THE PLAIN SENTENCE BESIDE THEM IN THE GLOSS** — the device Regiō
  VI used for the imperfect, X for the passive and XI for the
  subjunctive. **No new verb ever carries a new construction.** Every
  acc+inf here hangs on `videt` or `dīcit` (Regiō I); every ablative
  absolute is built on the passive Regiō X installed (`datur`,
  `pōnitur`, `ēicitur`, `vidētur`); both gerundives are on `dat` and
  `servat`, from Regiōnēs I and X.
* **THE ABLATIVE ABSOLUTE IS TAUGHT AS A PAIR, NEGATIVE THEN POSITIVE,
  ON ONE PICTURE-PAIR.** `Cibō nōn datō, corpus nōn valet` (f34 p14) and
  `Cibō datō, corpus iterum valet` (f34 p22) stand on mirror frames —
  the same man, the same table, turned away and then turned back. The
  learner meets the construction twice before being asked to do anything
  with it, and the second meeting IS the fable's resolution, so the
  grammar and the story turn on one hinge.
* **THE ACC + ĪNF IS ALWAYS ANSWERED BY THE PICTURE.** Indirect statement
  is the one construction a learner can parse correctly and still not
  know whether it is true, so all eleven here report something the frame
  shows: `lupus videt canem pinguem esse` stands over a fat dog.
* **B-RATING (DESIGN §8), two decisions, BOTH FLAGGED FOR LINE-AUDIT:**
  f35's snake TRIES AND FAILS — in the fable as commonly told the farmer
  is bitten and dies; here the snake `mordēre vult`, the farmer is
  quicker (`celeriter surgit`), NOBODY IS HARMED, and the snake goes
  back out into the snow. The mōrāle is untouched and is sharper for it:
  the ingratitude, not the injury, is the point, and `malus beneficium
  nōn reddit` says exactly that. f36's wolf is HUNGRY, not starving, and
  the collar is described, never shown as a wound.
* **THE WOLF ARC ENDS HERE, AND HE ENDS SYMPATHETIC.** The lamb-taker of
  Regiō I, who would not pay the crane in Regiō XI, is in f36 thin,
  alone, offered everything he wants — and turns it down. **He is never
  drawn in the 'angry' pose in this capitulum, not once**: the open-jawed
  wolf of eleven regions stands with his mouth shut through the whole of
  his last fable. The last thing a learner sees him do is choose.
* **THE LAST LINE OF THE TRACK** is f36's mōrāle, built entirely from
  words earned in four different regions: `Fābula docet: lībertās plūs
  quam cibus valet.` (`lībertās` R11 f32, where the frogs LOST it by
  asking for a king; `cibus` R4; `valet` f34 of this region; `plūs quam`
  the S8 comparative.) The wolf's own last words do the same with Regiō
  XI's punchline word — `“Ego ēsuriō, sed līber sum: id mihi satis est.”`
  `satis`, which in R11 was the wolf's excuse for paying nobody, is here
  what he settles for.
* **FORUM IS TAUGHT**, as TEMPLUM was in Regiō XI: `bgCity` with two
  `columna` and a `temple` in it is a forum, and `forum` is a card. The
  last two regions of the track are the only two that could picture
  their own names.

### THE BOSS — b_r12 LUPUS ULTIMUS, and two ENGINE CONSTRAINTS

Four phases where every other region has three, hp 10 over 90
phase-seconds, `bossMinMs` **20000** (not the 15000 every other duel
region uses — this fight is half again as long). The extra phase is a
SECOND CLĀMOR at the end, because the finale's job is to examine SYNTAX
and clāmor is the only phase that reads a sentence: the fight opens on
words (caterva), tests this region's own grammar (clāmor I), makes him
run (fuga), and finishes on the whole track's grammar (clāmor II).
`js/boss.js buildPlan()` iterates the list and does not deduplicate
types, and `js/boss-phases.js` prefers a phase's own `cfg.data.items`
over `boss.clamor`, so the two clāmor phases carry different sets and
never repeat a sentence. Both were verified against the real engine.

**CONSTRAINT 1 — THE BOSS CANNOT DRAW TRACK-WIDE, though a finale wants
to.** `js/app.js bossWords()` builds its pool from `CUR.region.capitula`
ONLY, and `runBossQuiz()` silently DROPS any entry it cannot resolve — so
`{ la: 'vulpēs', from: 'f1' }` would not fail loudly, it would just make
the quiz four questions long. `js/boss-phases.js fromAuthored()` resolves
clāmor answers and options against the same region-local pool and drops
what it cannot find (with a loud warning, at least). **The cumulative
feel was therefore built the legitimate way instead:** this region's own
cards deliberately carry words from six earlier regions — `cibus` R4,
`silva` R1, `canis` R2, `dominus`/`mēnsa`/`praesēpe` R6, `ignis` R10,
`templum`/`senex` R11 — so the boss draws on the whole track *through*
r12's own vocabulary, and every clāmor frame quotes an earlier region's
construction (R10's passive with the ā/ab agent, R11's purpose clause,
R9's relative clause, R8's comparative). FILED FOR THE ENGINE BACKLOG: a
`from` that could name a capitulum in an already-cleared region would let
a finale be a real cumulative exam.

**CONSTRAINT 2 — NO POST-WIN TEXT.** The finale wants to close on
`Lupus līber discēdit.` — the wolf walking away from his own boss fight
as he walks away from the collar. The engine has nowhere to put it:
`js/app.js showBossResult()` renders `DATA.MAP_UI.vicisti`, the mascot
and the XP bonus, and reads nothing from the region config. FILED FOR THE
ENGINE BACKLOG: an optional `boss.postWin` string rendered under the
victory line would cost one line in `showBossResult()` and would let
every region close in its own voice. The line is recorded here so it is
not lost.
