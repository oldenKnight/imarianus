words the learner already owns. **h58 introduces exactly one new word.**

## THE GRAMMAR THIS LIBER TEACHES

One kind at a time, each on a sentence whose vocabulary is already
owned, and each standing on **the Vulgate's own construction in that
very chapter**.

| where | target | the file's sentence | the verse it stands on |
|---|---|---|---|
| h51 | **purpose `ut` + subj.** | `Pāstōrēs in Bethlehem vēnērunt ut puerum vidērent.` | Lc 2,15–16 "Transeamus … et videamus" → "Et venerunt festinantes" |
| h52 | **`cum` + subj.** | `Cum Iesus in Bethlehem nātus esset, magī ab oriente vēnērunt.` | Mt 2,1 "**Cum ergo natus esset** Jesus in Bethlehem … magi ab oriente venerunt" |
| h52 | **`nē` + subj.** | `Angelus magōs monuit nē ad Herōdem redīrent.` | Mt 2,12 "**ne redirent** ad Herodem" |
| h52 | **ABLATIVE ABSOLUTE** | `Herōde mortuō, in terram Israēl rediērunt.` | Mt 2,19 "**Defuncto autem Herode**" |
| h53 | **indirect question** | `Maria et Ioseph quaerēbant ubi puer esset.` | Lc 2,45 "regressi sunt in Jerusalem, **requirentes eum**" |
| h54 | **purpose + PASSIVE** | `Tum Iesus ad Iordanem vēnit ut ā Ioanne baptizārētur.` | Mt 3,13 "venit Jesus … in Jordanem ad Joannem, **ut baptizaretur ab eo**" — the clause word for word in its construction |
| h55 | **result `tam … ut`** | `Ventus tam magnus erat ut nāvis aquā implērētur.` | Mt 8,24 "**ita ut** navicula **operiretur** fluctibus" |
| h56 | **ACC + INF (S12)** | `Pater fīlium venīre vīdit.` · `Parabola docet Deum hominēs amāre.` | Lc 15,20 "**vidit illum** pater ipsius" |
| h57 | abl. abs. recycled | `Cēnā factā, Iesus cum discipulīs in montem exiit.` | Mt 26,30 "**et hymno dicto**, exierunt in montem Oliveti" |
| h58 | abl. abs., verbatim | `Et discipulīs videntibus in caelum ascendit.` | Act 1,9 "**videntibus illis**, elevatus est" |

Every one is drilled ONCE, in its own capitulum's `complē`, on the h46
pattern (`Nunc Deus loquitur; tum Deus ad Nathan ___ est.`).

**NO FUTURE TENSE anywhere.** The future is on no rung of CURRICULUM §0
(Liber VIII settled this at 2 Rg 7,6–15) and this liber keeps the
discipline even where the Gospel's own verb is future — Lc 1,31's
"vocabis nomen ejus Jesum" is on no page, and Lc 2,12's "invenietis
infantem" is told in the present (`Puer in praesēpī est.`).
**NO PLUPERFECT** either, for the same reason: Mt 2,2's "vidimus" and
Mt 28,2's "revolvit" are the perfects the file writes.
**NO GERUNDIVE.** CURRICULUM §0 says "gerundive (light)"; no sentence in
these eight capitula wants one, and inventing an occasion for a
construction is the opposite of this method.

Every form verified in `latin-sources/vulgata-clementina-raw.txt`.

### NAZARETH IS NOT IN THIS FILE AT ALL — registered

Lc 2,4.39.51 and Mt 2,23 name it. Naming it would have cost a
proper-noun cap slot in **three separate capitula** for a place nothing
in the eight turns on, and the pages say `domum rediērunt` instead
(h52 p18, h53 p13). An omission, not an oversight.

### *** THE CROSS — THE RULING IN FULL ***

1. **THE CROSS IS DRAWN, AND IT IS DRAWN EMPTY.** This is not a choice
   made against the art library: `js/actors-props.js` documents the prop
   as *"crux — the empty cross on its little hill; dignified, **never a
   body**"*, and the actor has **no option, pose or flag that could put
   a figure on it**. The library decided; h57 conforms and says so.
2. **NO FIGURE OF OUR LORD APPEARS IN ANY CRUCIFIXION FRAME**, at a
   distance or otherwise. Between the arrest page and the empty cross
   there is no picture of him at all, and the next picture of him is
   h58's.
3. **THE TEXT IS THE VULGATE'S TWO MINIMAL CLAUSES AND NOTHING ELSE:**
   `Iesum crucifīxērunt. Iesus in cruce mortuus est.`
   — Mt 27,35 "**Postquam autem crucifixerunt eum**" for the first, and
   for the second the fixed formula Liber VI already shipped for Moyses
   (Dt 34,5), **`mortuus est`**, whose predicate the Vulgate itself
   applies to Christ (Rm 5,6 "Christus … pro impiis **mortuus est**";
   Rm 14,9 "Christus **mortuus est** et resurrexit"; 1 Cor 15,3).
   **Nothing is added to those two clauses.**
4. **NOT ON ANY PAGE, DRAWN OR TOLD:** nails, wounds, blood, the
   scourging, the crown of thorns, the mocking, the spitting, the blows,
   the two thieves, the reviling, the cry of dereliction, the sponge,
   the soldiers, the dice, the titulus, the lance. Every one is
   registered with its verse below.
5. **THE DARKNESS IS SHOWN**, by Liber VI's own device: the `nightSky`
   ground and a 🌑 bubble carried h32's plague of darkness (Ex 10,22)
   and carry Mt 27,45's here. `tenebrae` is FREE from h32 and is **not
   re-carded** — h32's own reasoning, that a word whose picture is a
   dark backdrop should never become a boss gap.
6. *** **`crux` AND `crucifīgit` ARE NOT VOCABULARY CARDS.** This is the
   third instance of a precedent this track has audited twice already —
   h21's `cornua` and h37's `sepelit`, whose ledger entry reads: *"a
   carded lexeme owes ≥3 recyclings and becomes eligible as a boss gap;
   **neither is a thing to do with a burial in a children's book**."*
   The reasoning is stronger here. Both words are taught on their page
   with the picture and a Latin gloss, listed here, and appear in **NO
   vocab card, NO lūdus pool, NO aenigmata pair, NO sonus set, NO
   corrige item, NO complē item, NO boss gap and NO boss distractor.**
   **The cross is never a falling tile.** h57's exercises are all
   hand-authored for exactly this reason: the derivers would have been
   within their rules to build a cloze on `Iesum crucifīxērunt` (it is a
   plain sentence with a scene and no direct speech), and the overrides
   exist so that they never get the chance. **This is the one place in
   Liber IX where LATIN-STYLE §2's ≥3-recycling rule is deliberately
   broken, and it is broken on purpose.** ***
7. **HIS MOTHER AND THE DISCIPLE STAND BESIDE THE CROSS, NOT AT A
   DISTANCE. — ASSIGNMENT CORRECTION, flagged.** The brief asked for
   "Mother and John at a distance". **Io 19,25 says "Stabant autem

   John in order to obey the brief. The page reads `Prope crucem māter

8. **THE DISCIPLE IS NOT NAMED.** Io 19,26 does not name him either

## Engine notes (still true in Liber IX)

1. **`grex` ignores `n`** — `js/actors-props.js` always draws three
   lambs. h21's device is the one this liber uses: **`ovis` is the
   `lamb` actor, ONE sheep**, and `grex` is the three.
2. **`crow`'s default pose carries a piece of cheese.** Any Historia
   page that wants a bird must pass `pose: 'sing'` or `'sad'`.
3. **`crowdGroup` ignores `pose` and `seed`** (Liber VII's finding, still
   true). Liber IX passes neither.
4. **`js/boss-phases.js`'s FUNCTIO stoplist** — Liber IX's eleven gaps
   are `praesēpe · stabulum · tūs · doctor · baptizat · nāvis · sānat ·
   seminat · ovis · cēna · sepulcrum`; none is on the list, and neither
   is any of their thirty-three distractors.
5. **`js/app.js` glossesFor reads `n.e || ''`** — the emoji field on a
   `nova` is optional, which is why `crux` and `crucifīxērunt` carry a
   Latin gloss and no glyph. **No cross glyph is used anywhere in this
   file**: ✝ appears in no shipped ledger, its rendering on the target
   stack is unverified, and the `crux` prop is a better picture than a
   glyph could be.
6. **`CONTENT.checkComple()` grades through `normWord()`, which STRIPS
   MACRONS** — so two options differing only by a macron are ONE word to
   the validator. `tests/regression.html` carries a named guard for it
   (BUG-4, "no shipped complē/corrige item has two options equal under
   macron-stripping"), and it sweeps only the regions the manifest
   declares, so it cannot see this liber until integration. **All 288 of
   Liber IX's complē/corrige options were run through the engine's own
   exported `CONTENT.normWord` and no pair collides.** h56's accusative +
   infinitive drill offers `venīre · venit · vēnērunt` — a present
   singular and a perfect PLURAL — and deliberately not `venit`/`vēnit`.
7. **`js/app.js` scramblePhase reverses an identity shuffle**, so a
   TWO-word scramble always displays the answer backwards and can be
   solved without reading the Latin; the engine's own deriver refuses any
   line under three tokens (`content-loader.js` usableLines: `n < 3 ||
   n > 9`). **Every authored scramble in this liber is 4–6 tokens**, the
   range Librī VI–VIII ship.
