# Vocabulary ledger — FĀBULAE track

NOT SHIPPED. This is the authoring instrument required by LATIN-STYLE §2 and
AUTHORING-BRIEF ("Working rules"): before introducing a "new" word, grep this
file. **If an earlier capitulum taught it, the word is FREE** — no `nova` gloss
needed and it does not count against the capitulum's ≤8 new lexemes.

Columns: `lexeme` — the citation form · `cap` — first use · `how` — the gloss
device that made it comprehensible (LATIN-STYLE §2: imāgō, `=` synōnymum,
`↔` contrārium, morphology pattern, or "context" when the sentence alone does it).

Three states are tracked deliberately:

* **TAUGHT** — appears in story text with a picture or a `nova` gloss. Free.
* **CONTEXT** — appears in story text with no gloss because the surrounding
  sentence and scene fully determine it (`est`, `et`, `in`…). Free.
* **GLOSS-ONLY** — the learner has only ever met it *inside a Latin gloss*
  (e.g. `quoque = etiam`). Weaker than TAUGHT. Listed separately because
  reusing one of these in a mōrāle — where no new word is permitted — is a
  judgement call, not an automatic yes.

## Regiō III · RĪVUS (S3) — `content/fabulae-r03.js`

Stage ceiling: everything S1–S2 **plus genitive and dative sg/pl**. No
ablative beyond the locative habit Regiōnēs I–II already ship (`in silvā`,
`in arbore`, `in agrō`, `in aquā`, and — new here, same shape — `in ōre`,
which is exactly Regiō I's own `in rōstrō tenet`). `ad` + acc as shipped.

Two rulings taken in this region, both recorded because a later author will
hit them again:

* **Ambiguous datives are avoided.** `rānae` is dat. sg. *and* nom. pl. Every
  dative in f8 is `bovī` (3rd decl.) or `rānīs` (dat. pl.); `rānae` appears
  only as a nominative plural, glossed as one.
* **The genitive is built from marked endings first.** `canis` is its own
  genitive, so the title's `umbra canis` is taught only after `rīvus → rīvī`
  and `umbra → umbrae` have shown what the case does.

## Regiō IV · MŌNS (S4) — `content/fabulae-r04.js`

Stage ceiling: everything S1–S3 **plus the ablative** — the six prepositions
`in / ex / ab / cum / dē / sub` and the instrumental ablative. Regiōnēs I–III
used `in silvā`-type locatives as an unanalysed habit; f10 p6 (`in monte`) is
where that habit is finally NAMED, and every later ablative is built on it.

Still forbidden and avoided throughout: imperative, vocative, question words,
pronouns, imperfect, perfect, comparative, and the infinitive with
`potest`/`vult`/`dēbet` (S5 per AUTHORING-BRIEF ruling 2 — so Regiō I's
`capere nōn potest` is NOT reused; every such thought is a plain negative
present instead).

**MISSING ART, reported not worked around:** there is no road/`via` prop or
background in the library, so `in viā` is not taught anywhere in this region.
The six prepositions the ladder actually names are all pictured instead.
